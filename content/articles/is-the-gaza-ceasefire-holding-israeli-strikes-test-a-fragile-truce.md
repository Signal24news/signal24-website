The **Gaza ceasefire** is still formally in place, but a string of Israeli strikes this week has raised fresh questions about whether it can hold.
 
An Israeli strike on a hospital in southern Gaza killed at least four people on Wednesday, 11 November, Gaza's civil defence said, [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/israeli-strike-southern-gaza-hospital-kills-four-gaza-civil-defence-says-2026-11-11/).
 
The same day, Israeli strikes hit a tent camp sheltering displaced families, and five people were killed in Gaza, [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/11/israeli-strikes-hit-tent-camp-as-five-killed-in-gaza).
 
## What each side says
 
Israel says its operations target Hamas fighters and threats to its troops, and accuses the group of trying to rebuild its military strength. Hamas accuses Israel of breaking the truce and has called on mediators and the United States to step in.
 
## Why the deal is stuck
 
The ceasefire took effect in October 2025. Its next phase depends on issues neither side has resolved: Hamas's disarmament, an Israeli military withdrawal, who will govern Gaza and how it will be rebuilt.
 
Israel insists Hamas must disarm first. Hamas has linked giving up its weapons to an Israeli withdrawal and to the deal being carried out in full.
 
## Life on the ground
 
Shortages of medical supplies and limits on aid groups are still making it hard to restore basic services, [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/six-year-old-gaza-burn-victim-caught-israeli-dispute-over-aid-2026-11-13/) on Friday.
 
For now, the truce has prevented a return to full-scale war. But with strikes continuing and talks on the next phase stalled, its future remains uncertain.
