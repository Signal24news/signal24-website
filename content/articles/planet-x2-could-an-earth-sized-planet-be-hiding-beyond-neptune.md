Planet X2 is the name researchers have given to a possible Earth-class planet orbiting far beyond Neptune. A study published in the [Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/doi/10.1093/mnras/stag2033/9003824) says such a world could help explain the unusual paths of distant icy objects. But Planet X2 has not been seen: it is a prediction from computer simulations, not a confirmed discovery.
 
## What the study found
 
The study was led by Patryk Sofia Lykawka of Kindai University in Japan, with Pedro Bernardinelli and Jonathan Horner, [Newsweek reported](https://tech.yahoo.com/science/articles/scientists-earth-sized-planet-could-114619507.html). The team ran simulations covering 4.5 billion years of Solar System history, with the four giant planets, a disc of icy bodies and Neptune's slow, uneven movement outward, according to [the paper](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/doi/10.1093/mnras/stag2033/9003824).
 
In the best-fitting models, Planet X2 has one to two times Earth's mass, with the strongest results for a planet twice Earth's mass. Its predicted orbit lies roughly 350 to 500 astronomical units (AU) from the Sun on average, and it comes no closer than about 200 AU, [the study says](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/doi/10.1093/mnras/stag2033/9003824). One AU is the average distance between Earth and the Sun.
 
## Why astronomers think something is there
 
Some objects beyond Neptune follow unusually stretched or tilted orbits that the known planets cannot easily explain. One example is Sedna, whose long orbit takes it far beyond the Kuiper Belt. The researchers tested whether an unseen planet's gravity could push icy bodies into these distant orbits, and found that models with Planet X2 [matched what we see more closely](https://ras.ac.uk/news-and-press/research-highlights/could-astronomers-be-closing-hidden-planet-beyond-neptune).
 
## How Planet X2 differs from Planet Nine
 
Planet X2 is not the same as the better-known Planet Nine idea. In the study's comparison, Planet X2 is an Earth-class object, while the Planet Nine model the team tested was larger, at about 6.2 Earth masses, with a less stretched orbit. The [Royal Astronomical Society](https://ras.ac.uk/news-and-press/research-highlights/could-astronomers-be-closing-hidden-planet-beyond-neptune) describes Planet X2 as a separate hypothesis. Neither planet has been found.
 
The model also has limits. The researchers report a mismatch between their simulations and the real spread of objects by orbit size, so the model needs more work. Matching several patterns does not prove the planet exists.
 
## How the Rubin Observatory could test it
 
The Vera C. Rubin Observatory in Chile could help. Its Legacy Survey of Space and Time will scan the sky again and again and find many more distant objects, whose orbits will show whether the patterns predicted by Planet X2 are real. The [University of Southern Queensland](https://www.unisq.edu.au/news/2026/11/hidden-planet) says Rubin's observations could provide important clues, and the survey may even spot the planet if it is bright enough. [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/new-dwarf-planet-rubin-pluto) has reported that Rubin data has already turned up a possible new dwarf planet beyond Neptune, 2026 ED138.
 
For now, Planet X2 remains a scientific possibility, not a newly confirmed planet.
