Tanker cyberattacks on as many as 50 commercial ships are being investigated by the US, which is examining whether Iran was behind them, [the Wall Street Journal reported](https://www.wsj.com/politics/national-security/iran-us-cyberattack-suspect-fed78942) on Monday, citing US officials. The ships were carrying oil, liquefied petroleum gas, chemicals and other hazardous goods, [according to Al-Monitor](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2026/11/us-eyes-iran-possible-culprit-dozens-new-cyberattacks-against-ships-wsj-reports), which said Reuters could not immediately verify the report.
 
Iran is the main suspect, but US authorities are also looking at whether [Russia could be responsible](https://abcnews.com/International/live-updates/iran-live-updates-tehran-strait-hormuz-safe-demands/?id=138369648), ABC News reported. Some ships received bomb threats warning that they would be blown up.
 
## How the ships were hacked
 
A US official told NPR that the attackers used [known weaknesses in Fortinet FortiGate devices](https://www.wusf.org/2026-11-24/the-u-s-government-is-investigating-iranian-ties-to-as-many-as-50-hacked-commercial-vessels), which manage firewalls and VPNs, to break in, then moved from the ships' IT networks to the systems that control physical operations. The official said this could have affected propulsion, steering, navigation and cargo systems, but gave no examples, and described the attacks as "not sophisticated."
 
The FBI and the US Coast Guard are investigating. The two agencies issued a [joint bulletin in September](https://www.wusf.org/2026-11-24/the-u-s-government-is-investigating-iranian-ties-to-as-many-as-50-hacked-commercial-vessels) warning that hackers were targeting unpatched FortiGate devices. Their press offices told NPR there are no current reports of operational disruptions, vessel instability, physical danger to crews or environmental impacts.
 
The Coast Guard says it helped [stop all the attacks](https://abcnews.com/International/live-updates/iran-live-updates-tehran-strait-hormuz-safe-demands/?id=138369648) and has urged tanker operators to patch their systems.
 
## The August boardings
 
The investigation follows the boarding of two Texas-bound tankers, the VL Prosperity and the Kohaku, by Coast Guard and FBI teams in the Gulf of Mexico on [21 and 24 August](https://dig.watch/updates/suspected-maritime-cyberattacks-security-concerns), after signs that their networks had been compromised. The VL Prosperity is a [Liberian-flagged crude tanker](https://www.securityweek.com/cyberattacks-on-two-oil-tankers-prompt-coast-guard-fbi-to-board-vessels/) that was heading from Egypt to Galveston, Texas.
 
Iran's Mehr news agency claimed at the time that the VL Prosperity [lost communications for 30 hours](https://therecord.media/oil-tanker-cyberattack-coast-guard-fbi). The Coast Guard confirmed signs of malicious cyber activity on the ship but [did not blame Iran](https://www.securityweek.com/cyberattacks-on-two-oil-tankers-prompt-coast-guard-fbi-to-board-vessels/).
 
## Attacks continue in the Strait of Hormuz
 
The cyber investigation comes as physical attacks on ships go on in the Gulf. The UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) centre said three vessels were [struck by projectiles in the Strait of Hormuz](https://abcnews.com/International/live-updates/iran-live-updates-tehran-strait-hormuz-safe-demands/?id=138369648) on Monday evening. All three continued their journeys and all crew were accounted for.
 
Separately, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy claimed that several tankers hit naval mines in the strait while travelling "illegally," [ABC News reported](https://abcnews.com/International/live-updates/iran-live-updates-tehran-strait-hormuz-safe-demands/?id=138369648), citing Iranian state media. US Central Command has called earlier IRGC mine claims false.
