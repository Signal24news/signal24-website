A dirty bomb is back in the headlines after CNN reported that new US intelligence indicates Iran is developing plans to target senior Trump administration officials with a dirty bomb or a chemical weapon in Washington, DC, [citing seven US officials](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/11/16/politics/us-intelligence-iran-dirty-bomb). The same officials disagree on whether Iran can or will try to carry out such a plot.
 
President Donald Trump said on Tuesday that the US was taking the possibility seriously, [Reuters reported](https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2026-11-17/tehran-denies-report-of-a-potential-iranian-plan-to-detonate-dirty-bomb-in-washington). "We're looking into it," he said, [according to USA Today](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/trump-says-us-taking-seriously-152142812.html). The White House has said the threat is not "credible", [Time reported](https://time.com/article/2026/11/17/iran-dirty-bomb-plot-radiological-chemical-weapon-washington-dc-intelligence/), and Tehran has denied the report.
 
Intelligence also reportedly intercepted Iranian chatter claiming bomb-making materials had been stashed inside the US, although officials disagree about how credible that claim is, [according to Time](https://time.com/article/2026/11/17/iran-dirty-bomb-plot-radiological-chemical-weapon-washington-dc-intelligence/). Since the US and Israel launched the war on Iran on 28 February, Iran has attacked US bases, energy sites and shipping in the region, but not the US mainland.
 
## What is a dirty bomb?
 
A dirty bomb, or radiological dispersal device, combines conventional explosives with radioactive material, [according to the US Nuclear Regulatory Commission](https://www.nrc.gov/regulations-legislation/fact-sheets-brochures/backgrounder-on-dirty-bombs). The blast spreads the radioactive material into the area around it.
 
It does not cause a nuclear explosion. Its main purpose is to spread contamination, damage property, disrupt daily life and cause fear. The NRC says most immediate injuries would come from the explosion itself, while radioactive dust or smoke could add a health risk if people breathe it in or swallow contaminated food or water.
 
## How is it different from a nuclear bomb?
 
A nuclear weapon releases enormous energy through a nuclear chain reaction, causing a huge blast, intense heat and possibly widespread radioactive fallout. A dirty bomb uses an ordinary bomb to scatter radioactive material and releases nothing close to that energy.
 
The difference matters because the damage from a dirty bomb would be local: contamination, evacuations, fear and an expensive clean-up, rather than mass destruction.
 
## Could Iran build one?
 
A dirty bomb does not need a nuclear weapon or weapons-grade uranium. Even enriched uranium is only weakly radioactive, which makes it a poor choice for a dirty bomb, [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/17/is-iran-plotting-a-dirty-bomb-attack-in-the-us-what-we-know).
 
Having a nuclear programme does not mean a country has built a radiological weapon. There is no public evidence that Iran has a dirty bomb or has obtained material for one, and the US assessments describe alleged planning, not a confirmed weapon or attack.
 
## What has the IAEA said about Iran?
 
The International Atomic Energy Agency said in a confidential report on 13 November that it was increasingly concerned about the Isfahan Fuel Enrichment Plant, which Iran has declared but inspectors have not been allowed to see, [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/iaea-increasingly-concerned-about-iranian-enrichment-plant-its-report-says-2026-11-13/). The agency said it does not know the plant's exact location, its status or whether it holds nuclear material.
 
An inspection had been planned for 13 June 2025, the day Israel began bombing Iranian nuclear sites. After the 12-day war that followed, Iran restricted IAEA access to the damaged sites. In June 2025 the IAEA Board of Governors also found Iran in breach of its safeguards obligations, [according to the Arms Control Association](https://www.armscontrol.org/factsheets/iaea-investigations-irans-nuclear-activities).
 
These concerns are about nuclear safeguards and verification. They are not evidence that Iran is building a dirty bomb.
 
## What about chemical weapons?
 
CNN's report also mentioned a possible chemical weapon. Earlier this month, Israel gave Washington intelligence suggesting Iran was considering chemical weapons, [The New York Times reported](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/03/world/middleeast/iran-chemical-weapons-israel-trump.html), though some US officials were sceptical of it. Iran's mission to the United Nations has called the chemical weapons claims false.
 
A dirty bomb spreads radioactive material. A chemical weapon spreads toxic chemicals. Neither has been confirmed as an Iranian attack plan.
 
## What should people do if one goes off?
 
The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention advises people near a radiation emergency to get inside, stay inside and stay tuned to official instructions, [as Time noted](https://time.com/article/2026/11/17/iran-dirty-bomb-plot-radiological-chemical-weapon-washington-dc-intelligence/). People who may have been exposed should remove their outer layer of clothing, seal it in a plastic bag and wash exposed skin with soap and water.
