US President Donald Trump often says he stopped the May 2025 conflict between India and Pakistan, and that planes were shot down during it. But the number he gives keeps changing. This page records every claim we could date and verify, and we will update it each time he says it again.

**Our verdict: Unsupported.** Trump has never said whose planes he means or where his figures come from, and his count has more than doubled in 15 months. Neither India nor Pakistan has backed any of his numbers.

The tariff figure has changed too. In a Fox News interview in October 2025 he spoke of a 200% threat. Days later, during his Asia trip, and again at his October 2026 rally, it was 250%.

## What India and Pakistan say

**India** has acknowledged losing aircraft but has not said how many. Its Chief of Defence Staff, General Anil Chauhan, told Bloomberg TV on 31 May 2025 that what mattered was "not the jet being down, but why they were being down", [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/5/31/india-top-general-admits-losses-in-recent-conflict-with-pakistan). On 9 August 2025, India's air force chief, Air Chief Marshal AP Singh, said India had shot down at least five Pakistani fighter jets and one large aircraft, [Business Standard reported](https://www.business-standard.com/external-affairs-defence-security/news/air-force-chief-confirms-5-pakistani-jets-downed-operation-sindoor-125080900603_1.html). India also rejects the idea that Trump brokered the ceasefire. It says the halt was agreed directly with Pakistan on 10 May 2025, after a request from Pakistan, [PTI noted](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/usa-news/trump-invokes-operation-sindoor-at-us-poll-rally-rues-nobel-snub).

**Pakistan** says it shot down five Indian planes and denies losing any of its own, [Reuters reported](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2608646). Pakistan has welcomed Trump's role. In June 2025, its government formally recommended him for the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize, crediting his "strategic diplomacy" for the ceasefire, [APP reported](https://www.app.com.pk/national/pakistan-recommends-u-s-president-donald-trump-for-2026-nobel-peace-prize/).

None of these figures has been independently confirmed.
