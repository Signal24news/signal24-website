The earthquake Los Angeles residents felt early on Friday, 20 November, struck near the city of Commerce in southeast Los Angeles County at 3:25am, waking people across Southern California, [ABC7 reported](https://abc7.com/post/magnitude-42-earthquake-strikes-commerce-area-usgs-says/20058854/). The US Geological Survey (USGS) first measured it at magnitude 4.2, raised it to 4.4, brought it back to 4.2 and finally settled on 3.9.
 
## What happened in Los Angeles?
 
The quake hit about 1 km northwest of Commerce at a depth of 15.5 km, according to the [USGS event page](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/ci41364111/executive), which now lists it as a reviewed magnitude 3.9. Commerce is about 7 miles from downtown Los Angeles, [CBS News reported](https://www.cbsnews.com/losangeles/news/earthquake-los-angeles-11-20-2026/).
 
People reported feeling it as far away as Lancaster, about 80 miles north of downtown LA, and Poway, about 115 miles to the south, [CBS News reported](https://www.cbsnews.com/losangeles/news/earthquake-los-angeles-11-20-2026/), citing the USGS. More than 13,000 people reported feeling the shaking on the USGS website, [according to NBC](https://www.nbcdfw.com/news/national-international/magnitude-3-9-earthquake-shakes-commerce-area/4108787/).
 
The USGS said the ShakeAlert early warning system was activated, [NBC Los Angeles reported](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/earthquakes/magnitude-3-9-earthquake-shakes-commerce-area/3973351/). No serious damage or injuries were reported in early checks.
 
## Why did the magnitude keep changing?
 
The changing numbers do not mean there were several earthquakes. The first figures are calculated automatically from the earliest seismic readings. As more data comes in, seismologists refine the quake's location, depth and size.
 
The USGS explains that different [magnitude types](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/science/magnitude-types) measure seismic waves in different ways, so early estimates and the final reviewed figure can differ. In this case the final figure, 3.9, is a "moment magnitude", which the USGS lists on its [event page](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/ci41364111/executive) as reviewed. That is why readers should check the time of a report and the latest USGS update rather than treat the first number as final.
 
## Why does Southern California get so many earthquakes?
 
Southern California sits on the boundary between the Pacific Plate and the North American Plate, which slowly slide past each other. Where rocks along a fault get stuck, stress builds up, and when they suddenly slip, the energy travels through the ground as seismic waves.
 
The San Andreas Fault is the best-known part of this boundary, but it is far from the only one. Southern California has more than 300 faults capable of producing magnitude 6 or larger earthquakes, [according to the USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/science/southern-california-earthquake-hazards).
 
## What should residents do?
 
Scientists cannot predict exactly when or where an earthquake will strike. The USGS advises people to know how to protect themselves during shaking, secure heavy furniture and keep basic emergency supplies ready.
