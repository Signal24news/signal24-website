A Houthi attack on Riyadh on Wednesday, 19 November, damaged a tower in the Saudi capital's King Abdullah Financial District, the Saudi-led coalition said, [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/houthi-missile-threat-towards-riyadh-causes-damage-saudi-led-coalition-says-2026-11-19/). Saudi civil defence said several floors of the building and nearby properties were damaged but no one was injured, [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/19/saudi-led-coalition-intercepts-houthi-missiles-targeting-riyadh-and-south).
 
## What happened in Riyadh?
 
State media images identified the building as the Public Investment Fund (PIF) Tower, which houses the headquarters of the kingdom's sovereign wealth fund, [according to Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/houthi-missile-threat-towards-riyadh-causes-damage-saudi-led-coalition-says-2026-11-19/).
 
The coalition said it had responded to a missile threat towards Riyadh and that debris fell in the financial district, [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/houthi-missile-threat-towards-riyadh-causes-damage-saudi-led-coalition-says-2026-11-19/).
 
## What Saudi Arabia and the Houthis say
 
Saudi Arabia's foreign ministry condemned attacks on civilian sites and said the kingdom reserved the right to take the necessary measures to defend its sovereignty, [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/19/saudi-led-coalition-intercepts-houthi-missiles-targeting-riyadh-and-south).
 
The Houthis said they targeted Riyadh's financial district twice on 19 November. They also claimed attacks on King Khalid International Airport, a southern air base and a petrochemical facility, but Saudi Arabia did not immediately confirm those claims, [according to Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/houthi-missile-threat-towards-riyadh-causes-damage-saudi-led-coalition-says-2026-11-19/).
 
## Why did Saudi-led forces strike Hodeidah?
 
On 20 November, the Saudi-led coalition said it struck Yemen's Red Sea port of Hodeidah, targeting facilities it said were used to smuggle weapons and ballistic missiles, after warning civilians to leave, [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/yemens-houthis-threaten-strike-saudi-red-sea-ports-2026-11-20/).
 
Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree then warned workers and shipping companies to leave Saudi Red Sea ports and urged airlines to avoid Saudi airspace, except around Mecca and Medina, [according to Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/yemens-houthis-threaten-strike-saudi-red-sea-ports-2026-11-20/).
 
## Were there casualties?
 
Saudi civil defence reported no injuries in Riyadh, [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/19/saudi-led-coalition-intercepts-houthi-missiles-targeting-riyadh-and-south). No confirmed casualty figure for the Hodeidah strike had been reported by [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/yemens-houthis-threaten-strike-saudi-red-sea-ports-2026-11-20/) at the time of writing.
 
## How does it link to the Iran war?
 
The Houthis are aligned with Tehran, while Saudi Arabia backs Yemen's internationally recognised government. Fighting in Yemen resumed in July alongside the wider Iran war, [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/houthi-missile-threat-towards-riyadh-causes-damage-saudi-led-coalition-says-2026-11-19/), with Houthi attacks on Saudi oil sites, airports and office buildings and Saudi-led strikes in Yemen.
 
Hodeidah is a key entry point for food and other goods into Houthi-held northern Yemen, so strikes on the port raise fears for aid supplies.
